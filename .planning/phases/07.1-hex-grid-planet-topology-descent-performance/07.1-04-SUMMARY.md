## Honest findings and caveats

- **Locate metric.** `locate` returns the nearest centre in the face-lattice (gnomonic planar) metric, which is the design's cell definition. Against the spherical nearest centre (max dot) it differs on 118 of 10,000 samples (1.18%, worst cosine gap 3.6e-5). This is expected near cell borders (gnomonic Voronoi differs from spherical Voronoi) and is not a failure, but selection/bounds code must use the lattice definition consistently.
- **Negative control is weak.** The "naive sum with zero terms" control also showed 0 edge mismatches. Adding two terms is commutative, so the invariant is nearly automatic once both faces share the same operands; only signed zeros could differ. The skip-zero-weight rule is therefore cheap insurance, not a discriminating test. The invariant was not shown to fail under any naive variant tried.
- **Area measure.** Measured with flat triangles on the unit sphere (kite per incident lattice triangle: centre, edge midpoint, triangle centroid). It reproduces the research numbers (1.52/1.87/1.96 at L3/5/7) to within 0.05 at comparable levels (1.565 at L3, 1.878 at L5). Pentagons are about half a mean hex, giving ~2.3-2.4 with them included.
- **Vertex wrap is not planar.** `canonical()` (diamond-plane unfolding) is defined for non-vertex cells within one step of the rhombus. The 12 pentagon cells use per-edge step enumeration instead (5 neighbours). Production code needs the same split.
- **Orientation.** The canonical `(0, +-1, +-phi)` icosahedron is kept unrotated (a rotation would not be exact-op); vertices 0/11 are the antipodal pair `(0,1,phi)` / `(0,-1,-phi)`. The "poles" are therefore not on a coordinate axis.
- **Not covered here:** S3 (coverage of half-cell pieces on the sphere), S4 (skirt gap on half diagonals). Those remain unanswered.

## Layout facts that held (useful for 07.1-10+)

- Diamonds: north `d=k` corners `[U_k, 0, L_k, U_{k+1}]`, south `d=5+k` corners `[L_k, U_{k+1}, 11, L_{k+1}]` as `[P00, P10, P01, P11]`; origin at an obtuse corner so each of the 20 diamond boundary edges is a near side of exactly one diamond and the 10 ring vertices are the 10 origins. Origin at an acute corner with half-open ownership would leave the 5+5 pole edges unowned.
- Parents: all-even barycentrics give 1 parent; otherwise the two ends of the coarse lattice edge (`w +- (1,-1)` on the two odd components, halved). Children = centre + level-(L+1) neighbours of the centre child.
- Wrap by reflection: crossing the edge opposite vertex C maps `(a, b, c<0)` to `(a+c, b+c, -c)` on the adjacent face.

## Deviations from Plan

- [Rule 3 - Blocking] Plan suggested neighbour directions `(+1,-1),(-1,+1)`; with 120 degree axes the correct diagonal is `(+1,+1),(-1,-1)` (plan explicitly allowed choosing the matching diagonal).
- Plan suggested wrap via a static table; implemented as a lazily built, cached `BTreeMap` per level keyed by a face-independent point key (also allowed by the plan), plus an independent diamond-plane unfold cross-check.
- Injectivity of cell directions is checked inside `s2_level_scaling_identical` so the suite has exactly 9 tests (5 s1_, 4 s2_).
