# ADR-0015: Scale, Contrast and Distance Haze (Generator v2, Surface Attribute v2)

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-26
- Requirement/Phase: PERF-01, PERF-02, HEX-02, HEX-03 / Phase 7.1

## Context

Two user reports from flying the descent: the planet "feels like a giant ball, not a planet" (no mid-scale relief between the 530 km base shape and the metre-scale detail, so nothing gives a sense of size) and "more contrast to pick things apart" (one narrow height-band palette, flat light, no distance cue). The fix has to stay generic where it touches the engine: planet wording belongs to the planetary extension, the render layer only knows a directional light, distance fog and a clear colour.

## Decision

1. Generator versioning. `TerrainParams.generator_version: u32` (1 or 2). `TerrainParams::default()` is version 1 so every existing core test and golden keeps exercising v1 bit for bit; `TerrainParams::demo_v2()` is the default plus `generator_version: 2` and the ridge fields (`ridge_amplitude 2500`, `ridge_frequency 12`, `ridge_octaves 5`: features of about 530 km down to 33 km). `TerrainConfig::default()` (planetary-terrain) uses `demo_v2()`, so the app and the descent suites run v2. `validate()` rejects versions outside {1, 2} and, for v2 only, a non-finite or non-positive ridge amplitude or frequency, ridge octaves outside `1..=24` and `frequency * 2^octaves >= 2^40` (ridge fields are ignored for v1).
2. Ridge layer. A new layer `planet.ridge` with `RIDGE_LAYER_VERSION = 2` (seed `layer_seed(world, "planet.ridge", 2)`; base, detail and mask keep layer version 1 and their seeds). `r = 1 - |2 fbm - 1|`, `ridge = A_r r r`, and `compose_v2 = max(sea, base + detail w + ridge w)` with `w = 0.25 + 0.75 mask`. Only `+ - * /` and `abs`, inside the border-critical region (ADR-0008). The cube `LayerStack` and the hex pointwise `sample` produce bit-identical heights.
3. Selection bounds. `LodModel.height.max` uses `TerrainParams::max_relief()` (adds `A_r` for v2) and `residual[L]` adds `A_r (4 V_r + 0.75 V_m)`: `r` is 2-Lipschitz in the fbm value, `r <= 1` makes `r r` 4-Lipschitz, the mask weight is at most 1 and varies by `0.75 V_m` while `r r <= 1`. A property test checks `|h(p) - h(q)| <= residual[L]` for 2000 seeded direction pairs at distance `theta(L)` for levels 1 to 12, for both the cube and the hex sample angle.
4. Surface attribute v2 (generator v2 only). `uv.x` stays the normalised height. `uv.y` becomes one ramp scalar: `q = 0.4 (1 - mask) + 0.6 rock_t`, texel row `(0.5 + 7 q) / 8`, with `slope = 1 - n . d` (unit normal and unit direction, f64 `+ - * /`) and `rock_t = clamp((slope - LO) / (HI - LO), 0, 1)`. A single scalar interpolates cleanly under linear filtering. v1 keeps `uv.y = mask`. The debug palette still overrides `uv.y` with the per-cell selector.
5. Rock thresholds are calibrated, not the planned 0.05 and 0.2. `1 - cos(angle)` is quadratic in the inclination: 0.05 is about 18 degrees, and this terrain's amplitudes over its wavelengths give gradients of about 1 percent. Measured land-vertex slopes (400 seeded patches per level, `measure_real_slopes`): level 4 p50 3.1e-6, p90 1.2e-5, p99 2.5e-5; level 8 p50 1.0e-5, p90 4.0e-5, p99 8.7e-5; level 12 p50 1.1e-5, p90 4.4e-5, p99 1.1e-4; level 16 p50 1.4e-5, p90 5.0e-5, p99 1.1e-4; level 20 p50 1.3e-5, p90 5.4e-5, p99 1.0e-4; maximum below 2e-4 everywhere. With the planned values the ramp was inert (`rock_t` identically 0, palette rows 3 to 7 unreachable). `ROCK_SLOPE_LO = 1e-5` and `ROCK_SLOPE_HI = 1e-4` put rock on the upper half of land vertices at fine levels and full rock on about the steepest 1 to 2 percent; `rock_ramp_is_not_inert_on_real_land` guards this. The slope comes from the mesh normal and therefore depends on the level: coarse meshes average slope away, so from altitude there is little rock and it appears during the descent. This is accepted.
6. Morph targets carry the colour. The morph attribute (ADR-0014) carries the ramp row like any uv, but the ramp depends on the level through the normal. A child vertex that coincides with a parent vertex therefore takes the parent level's ramp, not its own. For an interior parent vertex of the cube the parent's own stencil is the child vertices two cells away, so no extra samples are needed (measured: 553 of 600 coincident vertices bit-equal to the parent mesh colour, worst row difference 0.016 of a 0.125 row step on the rest); border vertices and the hex path use the face-independent border stencil of the parent level (about 4 extra terrain samples per vertex; no measurable cost on hex). A first version that re-sampled all coincident cube vertices doubled the cube stream time (frames-to-detail 75 to 173) and was replaced.
7. Palette v2. `palette_rgba8_v2()`: 64 x 8 texels, row `r` is `q = r / 7`. Bands sand (222,200,140), green (58,128,52) over a 14 column blend (no hard edge flipping under sub-metre changes), olive-brown (128,104,60), rock (120,118,116), snow (250,250,255). Rows 0 to 3 go lush to dry, rows 3 to 7 blend to rock grey with weight `(5 r - 14) / 21`, fading out on the snow columns. Integer-only. The v1 and debug palettes are unchanged and pinned by an FNV test; `TerrainSystem::setup` picks the palette from `generator_version`.
8. Relief lighting and haze policy lives in the app and uses only the generic render API. The sun is placed 35 degrees above the local horizon at the scripted descent target (`sun_direction_for_target`, pure and deterministic), intensity 1.8, ambient 0.025. `haze_for_altitude`: `DistanceFog` density 1e-5 per metre (half haze at 100 km) below 20 km altitude, fading linearly to 0 at 200 km; the clear colour is the sky colour times the same fade plus the space colour times the rest, so the horizon fog colour equals the sky behind it. A non-finite altitude gives no haze. `AppSettings.haze = false` leaves the fog and clear colour at their defaults. Values are first guesses to be judged by eye (Pending, human).
9. Precursor, not the atmosphere. The distance haze is a stand-in so that scale reads before Phase 8. PLAN-08 (atmosphere with scattering) supersedes it; `DistanceFog` stays a generic render feature of the engine and is not removed.

## Alternatives Considered

- Shader-side slope colouring (slope from screen-space derivatives of the interpolated normal): rejected; it needs a new shader path and a second texture lookup, and the CPU already has the normal.
- A 2D palette lookup (height by mask, plus a second texture for rock): rejected; two attributes and two samples instead of one ramp scalar, and the uv budget is already used by the morph attribute.
- Putting the ridge into the existing detail layer: rejected; it would change v1 heights and every golden. A new versioned layer leaves v1 untouched.
- Keeping the planned slope thresholds: rejected, inert on this terrain (see 5).

## Consequences

- Generator cost: every terrain sample adds a 5-octave fbm (about 23 percent more noise octaves). Worker mesh time grows accordingly; hex, which is already worker-bound, shows it directly (see Evidence). The wider relief bound also keeps more nodes alive in selection.
- v2 heights, meshes and the default palette differ from v1; v1 goldens are kept as `*_v1` tests and still pass. v2 goldens are new.
- Rock appears only at fine levels; from orbit the planet is coloured by height band and mask.
- The shading normal and colour of a morphing hex cell use an approximation of the parent's normal (border stencil rather than the parent's interior stencil); not separately tested for hex.

- Goldens: v1 `GOLDEN_HEIGHTS_V1 0xf067ef3fb83fbd54`, `GOLDEN_DIRS 0x3697d55ac59d0aa2` unchanged; v2 `GOLDEN_HEIGHTS_V2 0x3096173351c359fb` (recorded with `cargo test -p planetary-core --test generator goldens_match_at_1_and_4_threads_v2 -- --nocapture`), identical at 1 and 4 threads.
- Bounds: `v2_global_range_contains_samples` (20000 directions), `v2_residual_bound_contains_fine_samples`, `v2_relief_bound_over_direction_pairs_cube_and_hex` pass.
- Release `--bench-descent --frames 600` (worst band `<100 m`, p95, budget 16.7 ms): v1 baseline hex 11.0 ms, cube 1.41 ms; v2 hex 13.7 to 15.4 ms (5 runs: 14.78, 14.23, 14.26, 15.42, 13.73), cube 1.63 to 1.82 ms (all PASS). Max nodes visited in the run: hex 907 (v1) and 1221 (v2), cube 562 and 730 to 874; max held hex 1625 and 1517, cube 679 and 346 (v2 workers finish fewer meshes in the same frames); max wanted is capped at 900 in both.
- Release `--bench-stream --fast` (frames-to-detail / convergence): v1 hex 167 / 231, cube 76 / 55; v2 hex 206 / 290, cube 80 / 60.
